# Working Safely Around Our Robots — Customer Summary

A one-page summary written for customer staff. The authoritative rules are in OPS-003; where this page and OPS-003 differ, OPS-003 wins.

## The short version

Our robots are designed to stop for people. They see the world with a laser scanner and cameras, and they will slow down and stop rather than push past you. You do not need to get out of their way, and they will not try to squeeze past you.

That said, they cannot see through racking. Coming out of an aisle without looking is the one situation where the robot may not have room to stop in time.

## What to wear

A high-visibility vest, always. Closed shoes. If you have not done our short safety course, you need someone with you.

## Stopping a robot

There are big red buttons on both sides of every robot. Press one and it stops immediately. There are also larger buttons at the ends of the aisles, and pull cords along the picking faces, which stop every robot in that area at once.

Once a robot has been stopped this way, someone trained has to go to the robot and reset it. It cannot be restarted from a computer.

## Keeping things moving

The single biggest cause of slow robots is clutter. Pallets, shrink wrap and cages left in an aisle make the robots take long detours. Keep the aisles clear and the fleet runs at full speed.

## If something happens

Tell your supervisor and tell us, the same day, even if nobody was hurt and even if you are not sure anything actually happened. We would much rather hear about ten non-events than miss one real one.
