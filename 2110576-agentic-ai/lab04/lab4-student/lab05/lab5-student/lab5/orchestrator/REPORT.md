## What this report does not claim

What the agent was told and did not verify. One line each.

- I never saw a workbook. Every number above came back from `./wall`, which runs the team's tool on the server; I could not open a file to check any of it.
- TOI 11 and TOI 17 have no medal column. Their medals were derived from the score column, and derived medals are what feeds their rows in questions a, b and c.
- TOI 11 was derived by counting the scores at or above each cutoff the workbook itself declares. That the workbook's cutoffs are the cutoffs actually used is the workbook's claim, not something I checked.
- TOI 11's derivation agrees with itself from both ends — counting down from the declared cutoffs and ranking up from the declared counts give the same 13/16/21 — but both ends start from the same summary block, so the agreement is consistency, not confirmation.
- TOI 11's summary block also holds a second column of numbers, 8/16/21, beside the 13/16/21 the tool used. I do not know what the 8 means and did not use it.
- TOI 17 was derived the other way: the workbook declares 8, 15 and 22 medals, so the cutoffs above are the 8th, 23rd and 45th highest scores. That those counts are right is the workbook's claim.
- TOI 21's rows and TOI 21's own summary block disagree: counting the medal column gives 10 gold, 19 silver, 24 bronze; the workbook's summary says 8, 15 and 23. The table above reports the rows. I could not find out which is correct.
- TOI 18 has three contestants labelled "equivalent to gold" and "equivalent to silver". They are excluded from the counts, matching how TOI 18's own summary block counts them. Whether that is right for this report I did not verify.
- TOI 20 has four contestants marked as IPST students rather than medallists; they are excluded, again matching the workbook's own total of 47.
- TOI 16's sheet holds 47 rows and every one of them has a medal. It appears to be a list of medallists rather than of contestants, so its cutoffs are sound but nothing can be said about the size of the field.
- A cutoff for a small medal group is one contestant's own score. Every gold cutoff above rests on a group of 7 to 13 people.
- In the years whose workbook has no centre column, a medal's centre came from the code inside the contestant's username. That the code names the centre that entered them is an assumption.
- Some centre labels and username codes were placed by the local model choosing from the fixed list of seventeen centres. The tool accepts only a value from that list and discards anything else the model says, but a wrong choice from the list would be silent.
- Question c matches people between years by their identity string after normalisation, compared as a salted hash. Two different people with the same normalised string count as one; one person written differently in two years counts as two. Neither can be checked without reading the names, which I may not do.
- The counts above are of medals, not of medallists: a person who won two medals in different years is two rows in question b and one person in question c.
